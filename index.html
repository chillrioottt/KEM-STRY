Chapter-Wise Curriculum Summaries

Comprehensive NCERT & StudyRankers-aligned conceptual breakdown for every single chapter from Class 8 through 12.

CLASSES 8–12 UNIFIED

Physical Characteristics: Metals are malleable (can be beaten into thin sheets, Gold/Silver are most malleable), ductile (drawn into wires), sonorous (ring when struck), and good conductors of heat and electricity. Non-metals (Carbon, Sulfur, Phosphorus) are brittle and poor conductors (graphite is an exception).

Chemical Properties: Metals react with oxygen to yield basic oxides (e.g., $2Mg + O_2 \to 2MgO$; turns red litmus blue). Non-metals form acidic oxides ($S + O_2 \to SO_2$, producing sulfurous acid $H_2SO_3$ in water). Metals react with dilute acids to produce hydrogen gas ($H_2$ pops with a burning splinter), while non-metals generally do not.

Displacement Protocol: A more reactive metal replaces a less reactive metal from its aqueous salt solution ($Fe + CuSO_4 \to FeSO_4 + Cu$; blue solution turns pale green).

Exhaustible Natural Resources: Fossil fuels formed over millions of years through carbonisation (slow conversion of dead vegetation into coal under extreme heat and pressure).

Fractional Distillation of Petroleum: Petroleum is separated based on boiling point differentials: Petroleum gas (LPG), Petrol, Kerosene (jet fuel), Diesel, Lubricating oil, Paraffin wax, and Bitumen (used for road surfacing).

Destructive Distillation of Coal: Heating coal in the absence of air yields Coke (almost pure carbon), Coal Tar (thick liquid used in dyes/drugs), and Coal Gas (fuel).

Conditions for Combustion: 1. Fuel, 2. Air (Oxygen), 3. Ignition Temperature (minimum temperature to catch fire). The fire triangle dictates that removing any of these three extinguishes the fire.

Flame Architecture: 1. Innermost Zone (unburnt wax vapors, dark, least hot), 2. Middle Zone (partial combustion, yellow, luminous, moderately hot), 3. Outermost Non-luminous Zone (complete combustion, blue, hottest part, goldsmiths use this zone).

Calorific Value: Heat produced by complete combustion of 1 kg of fuel (kJ/kg). Hydrogen holds the highest calorific value ($150,000 \text{ kJ/kg}$).

Kinetic Molecular Theory: Matter particles possess kinetic energy, continuous motion, and intermolecular forces. Solid > Liquid > Gas in intermolecular forces; Gas > Liquid > Solid in kinetic energy.

Latent Heat: Latent heat of fusion (heat required to turn 1 kg solid to liquid at melting point) and Latent heat of vaporization (liquid to gas at boiling point without temperature change). Steam causes more severe burns than boiling water at 100°C due to excess latent heat.

Evaporation Factors: Rate increases with surface area, temperature, and wind speed; decreases with humidity. Evaporation causes cooling.

Classification: Pure Substances (Elements and Compounds) vs. Mixtures (Homogeneous solutions and Heterogeneous suspensions/colloids).

Colloids & Tyndall Effect: Heterogeneous mixtures with particle size between $1\text{ nm}$ and $1000\text{ nm}$. They scatter light (Tyndall effect) and do not settle under gravity. Examples: milk, fog, smoke.

Concentration Expressions: $\text{Mass \%} = (\text{Mass of solute} / \text{Mass of solution}) \times 100$.

Fundamental Laws: 1. Law of Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier), 2. Law of Definite Proportions (Proust — in a chemical substance, elements combine in fixed ratio by mass, e.g., $H_2O$ is always $1:8$).

Mole Concept: $1 \text{ mole} = 6.022 \times 10^{23} \text{ particles} (N_A)$. Molar mass in grams equals atomic/molecular mass in unified atomic mass units (u). $n = m / M = N / N_A$.

Atomic Models Evolution: Thomson's Plum Pudding $\to$ Rutherford's $\alpha$-particle scattering (discovered positive, dense nucleus) $\to$ Bohr's discrete energy levels ($K, L, M, N$ shells with capacity $2n^2$).

Isotopes vs. Isobars: Isotopes have identical atomic number ($Z$) but different mass numbers ($A$), e.g., $^{35}_{17}Cl$ and $^{37}_{17}Cl$. Isobars have identical mass number ($A$) but different atomic numbers ($Z$), e.g., $^{40}_{18}Ar$ and $^{40}_{20}Ca$.

Reaction Taxonomies: Combination, Decomposition (thermal, electrolytic, photolytic), Displacement, Double Displacement (precipitation & neutralization), and Redox (oxidation is loss of $e^-$ / gain of $O$; reduction is gain of $e^-$ / gain of $H$).

Oxidative Degradation: Corrosion ($4Fe + 3O_2 + 2xH_2O \to 2Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O$, rust) and Rancidity (aerial oxidation of fats and oils prevented using nitrogen gas or antioxidants like BHA).

Arrhenius Fundamentals: Acids produce $H^+(aq)$ / $H_3O^+$ ions; bases produce $OH^-(aq)$ ions. $pH = -\log_{10}[H^+]$. Range: 0 to 14.

Industrial Salts:

  • Bleaching Powder: $Ca(OH)_2 + Cl_2 \to CaOCl_2 + H_2O$.
  • Baking Soda: $NaCl + H_2O + CO_2 + NH_3 \to NH_4Cl + NaHCO_3$.
  • Washing Soda: $Na_2CO_3 \cdot 10H_2O$.
  • Plaster of Paris: $CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O$ (obtained by heating gypsum at 373 K).

Reactivity Series: $K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au$.

Ionic Bonding: Electrostatic attraction formed by complete valence electron transfer (e.g., $Na \to Na^+ + e^-$, $Cl + e^- \to Cl^-$). Ionic solids exhibit high melting points, brittle crystal lattices, and conduct electricity only in molten or aqueous states.

Metallurgy: Roasting (sulfide ores heated in excess air) vs. Calcination (carbonate ores heated in limited air). Thermite process: $Fe_2O_3 + 2Al \to 2Fe + Al_2O_3 + \text{heat}$.

Covalent Tetravalency: Carbon shares 4 electrons to form stable octets. Catenation allows self-linking into chains, branches, and rings.

Homologous Series: Successive members differ by $-CH_2-$ unit ($14\text{ u}$). Alkanes ($C_nH_{2n+2}$), Alkenes ($C_nH_{2n}$), Alkynes ($C_nH_{2n-2}$).

Soaps & Micelles: Sodium/potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids. Hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail binds grease; hydrophilic carboxylate head interacts with water.

Stoichiometry & Limiting Reagent: The reactant consumed entirely limits product yield.

Concentration Expressions:

  • Molarity ($M$): $\text{Moles of solute} / \text{Volume of solution in L}$ (Temperature dependent).
  • Molality ($m$): $\text{Moles of solute} / \text{Mass of solvent in kg}$ (Temperature independent).
  • Mole Fraction ($x_A$): $n_A / (n_A + n_B)$.

Wave Mechanics: de Broglie wavelength $\lambda = h / mv$; Heisenberg Uncertainty $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge h / 4\pi$.

Quantum Numbers: Principal ($n$), Azimuthal ($l = 0 \dots n-1$), Magnetic ($m_l = -l \dots +l$), Spin ($m_s = \pm 1/2$).

Orbital Rules: Aufbau Principle ($1s \to 2s \to 2p \dots$), Pauli Exclusion (no two electrons have identical 4 quantum numbers), Hund's Rule (maximum multiplicity in degenerate orbitals).

Periodic Trends:

  • Atomic Radius: Decreases across period (increasing $Z_{eff}$); increases down group.
  • Ionization Enthalpy: Anomalies at $Be > B$ (full $2s^2$) and $N > O$ (half-filled $2p^3$).
  • Electron Gain Enthalpy: Chlorine has more negative $\Delta_{eg}H$ than Fluorine due to Fluorine's compact $2p$ subshell having strong inter-electronic repulsions.

VSEPR & Hybridisation: Molecular geometries dictated by lone pair/bond pair repulsion ($LP-LP > LP-BP > BP-BP$). $sp$ (linear, 180°), $sp^2$ (trigonal planar, 120°), $sp^3$ (tetrahedral, 109.5°), $sp^3d$ (trigonal bipyramidal), $sp^3d^2$ (octahedral).

Molecular Orbital Theory: Bond Order $= \frac{1}{2}(N_b - N_a)$. Diamagnetic if all paired; paramagnetic if unpaired electrons exist (e.g., $O_2$ has 2 unpaired electrons in $\pi^*2p$ orbitals).

First & Second Laws: $\Delta U = q + w$; Enthalpy $H = U + pV$.

Spontaneity & Gibbs Energy: $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$. A reaction is spontaneous when $\Delta G < 0$. At equilibrium, $\Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K$.

Le Chatelier's Principle: A system at equilibrium responds to stress (concentration, temperature, pressure) by shifting in the direction that counteracts the perturbation.

Ionic Equilibrium: Henderson-Hasselbalch buffer equation $pH = pK_a + \log([\text{Salt}]/[\text{Acid}])$. Solubility Product $K_{sp}$; precipitation occurs when Ionic Product ($Q_{sp}$) exceeds $K_{sp}$.

Oxidation Number Tracking: Rules for calculating formal oxidation states. Balancing via Ion-Electron method in acidic ($H^+/H_2O$) and alkaline ($OH^-/H_2O$) media.

Electronic Displacement Effects: Inductive ($+I, -I$), Electromeric ($E$), Resonance ($+R, -R$), and Hyperconjugation ($\sigma$-$\pi$ conjugation). Carbocation stability: $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \text{CH}_3^+$.

Purification Methods: Fractional distillation, steam distillation, and chromatography ($R_f$ value).

Alkanes: Free radical halogenation mechanism. Wurtz reaction: $2RX + 2Na \to R-R + 2NaX$.

Alkenes & Alkynes: Markovnikov's Addition (electrophile adds to carbon with more hydrogens) vs. Anti-Markovnikov Kharasch Peroxide Effect ($HBr + \text{peroxide}$). Ozonolysis cleavage produces carbonyl compounds.

Aromaticity: Hückel's Rule ($4n + 2$ $\pi$-electrons in a planar ring). Electrophilic aromatic substitution: nitration, halogenation, Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation.

Raoult's Law: $p_1 = p_1^\circ x_1$. Ideal solutions exhibit $\Delta H_{mix} = 0, \Delta V_{mix} = 0$. Positive deviation (weaker solute-solvent interaction, e.g., Ethanol + Acetone); Negative deviation (stronger solute-solvent hydrogen bonds, e.g., Chloroform + Acetone).

Colligative Properties & van 't Hoff Factor: $\Delta T_b = i K_b m$; $\Delta T_f = i K_f m$; $\Pi = i C R T$. For dissociation: $\alpha = (i - 1) / (n - 1)$.

Nernst Formulation: $E_{cell} = E^\circ_{cell} - \frac{0.0591}{n} \log Q$ at 298 K. $\Delta G^\circ = -n F E^\circ_{cell} = -RT \ln K_c$.

Conductivity: $\kappa = \frac{1}{R} \cdot \frac{l}{A}$; $\Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa \times 1000}{M}$. Kohlrausch's Law: $\Lambda^\circ_m = \nu_+ \lambda^\circ_+ + \nu_- \lambda^\circ_-$.

Integrated Rate Laws: Zero Order: $k = ([R]_0 - [R]) / t$, $t_{1/2} = [R]_0 / 2k$. First Order: $k = \frac{2.303}{t} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]}$, $t_{1/2} = 0.693 / k$.

Arrhenius Equation: $k = A e^{-E_a / RT}$; $\log \frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303R} \left( \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1 T_2} \right)$.

3d Series Highlights: Variable oxidation states, catalytic behavior, alloy formation, colored ions via $d$-$d$ transitions. Positive $E^\circ(Cu^{2+}/Cu) = +0.34\text{ V}$.

Lanthanoid Contraction: Ineffective $4f$ nuclear shielding causes continuous radius decrease, rendering $Zr$ (160 pm) $\approx$ $Hf$ (159 pm) radii almost identical.

Oxidizing Reagents: $K_2Cr_2O_7$ (dichromate) and $KMnO_4$ (permanganate; intense color is due to Ligand-to-Metal Charge Transfer, not $d$-$d$ transition).

Crystal Field Theory (CFT): Splitting of $d$-orbitals in octahedral ($\Delta_\circ$, $t_{2g}$ vs. $e_g$) and tetrahedral fields ($\Delta_t = \frac{4}{9}\Delta_\circ$).

Spectrochemical Series: $I^- < Br^- < Cl^- < F^- < OH^- < H_2O < NH_3 < en < CN^- < CO$. Strong field ligands induce pairing ($P < \Delta_\circ$). Spin-only formula: $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\text{ BM}$.

Nucleophilic Substitution: $S_N2$ (bimolecular, single-step backside attack, complete Walden inversion, order: $1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$) vs. $S_N1$ (unimolecular, carbocation intermediate, racemization, order: $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$).

Haloarene Inertness: Low reactivity toward nucleophiles due to resonance partial double-bond character of $C-Cl$ and $sp^2$ carbon hybridization.

Key Reactions: Hydroboration-oxidation (anti-Markovnikov hydration), Lucas Test ($ZnCl_2 + \text{conc. } HCl$: $3^\circ$ alcohol produces turbidity instantly).

Phenols: More acidic than alcohols due to phenoxide resonance. Kolbe's Reaction (Salicylic acid) and Reimer-Tiemann Reaction (Salicylaldehyde with $CHCl_3 + NaOH$). Williamson Ether Synthesis ($R-X + R'-ONa \to R-O-R'$).

Carbonyl Reactivity: Nucleophilic addition across $>C=O$.

Distinction Tests: Tollens' mirror test and Fehling's red $Cu_2O$ test (positive for aldehydes, negative for ketones). Iodoform test for $CH_3-C=O$.

Named Reactions: Aldol Condensation ($\alpha$-hydrogen present) vs. Cannizzaro Reaction (no $\alpha$-hydrogen; disproportionation in conc. $NaOH$). Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) $\alpha$-halogenation.

Basicity: In aqueous solution: $(CH_3)_2NH > CH_3NH_2 > (CH_3)_3N > NH_3$. Aniline is a weaker base due to nitrogen lone pair delocalization into the aromatic ring.

Named Syntheses: Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis (pure aliphatic $1^\circ$ amines only); Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation ($R-CONH_2 + Br_2 + 4KOH \to R-NH_2 + K_2CO_3$, descent of carbon chain). Carbylamine test ($1^\circ$ amines yield foul isocyanide).

Carbohydrates: D-Glucose open-chain vs. $\alpha$/$\beta$-anomers; glycosidic linkage in sucrose (non-reducing), starch, cellulose.

Proteins & Nucleic Acids: $\alpha$-amino acids linked via peptide bonds ($-CO-NH-$). Denaturation disrupts $2^\circ$ and $3^\circ$ structures while leaving primary sequence intact. DNA (double-helix with Adenine=Thymine, Guanine≡Cytosine) vs. RNA (Uracil replaces Thymine).

Syllabus Conceptual Integration

Class 10: Demonstrates tetravalency ($C$ atomic number 6, configuration 2,4). Sharing 4 electrons satisfies octet.
Class 11: $2s$ mixes with three $2p$ orbitals yielding 4 equivalent $sp^3$ degenerate hybrid orbitals. Forms 4 $\sigma$-bonds (e.g., Methane $CH_4$, Ethane $C_2H_6$).
Class 12: Tetrahedral $sp^3$ carbon with four distinct substituents is chiral (asymmetric), leading to optical activity and inversion in $S_N2$ nucleophilic substitutions.
CLASS 10 FOUNDATIONS

Catenation & Allotropy

Carbon forms millions of stable compounds due to two unique attributes:

  • Catenation: Strong $C-C$ bond energy ($348 \text{ kJ/mol}$) allows formation of long open and closed rings.
  • Tetravalency: Capability to bond with hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, and halogens.
  • Allotropes: Diamond (3D $sp^3$ rigid tetrahedral network; insulator, hardest substance), Graphite (2D $sp^2$ hexagonal sheets with delocalized electrons; conducts electricity, lubricant), and Buckminsterfullerene ($C_{60}$ soccer ball cage).
CLASS 11 MECHANISMS

Electronic Effects & Intermediates

Electronic factors dictating reactivity in organic reactions:

  • Inductive Effect: Permanent dipole transmission along a $\sigma$-chain ($-I$: $-NO_2 > -CN > -F > -Cl$; $+I$: $-C(CH_3)_3 > -CH(CH_3)_2 > -CH_3$).
  • Resonance / Mesomeric: Delocalization of $\pi$-electrons ($+R$ activates benzene ring towards electrophiles, e.g., $-OH, -NH_2$).
  • Carbocation Stability: $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$ governed by hyperconjugation ($\alpha$-C-H bonds) and $+I$ dispersion of positive charge.
CLASS 12 ADVANCED

SN1 vs SN2 & Named Syntheses

High-yield mechanistic distinctions and named organic reactions:

  • SN1: Two steps via planar carbocation; leads to racemization ($3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$).
  • SN2: One concerted step via pentacoordinate transition state; 100% Walden inversion ($1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$).
  • Aldol vs Cannizzaro: Aldehydes with $\alpha$-H form $\beta$-hydroxyaldehydes (Aldol); aldehydes without $\alpha$-H undergo disproportionation (Cannizzaro).
